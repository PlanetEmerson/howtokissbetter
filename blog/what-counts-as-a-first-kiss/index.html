What Counts as a First Kiss? (The Cheek, the Peck, the Dare)

What counts as a first kiss? Lips on lips, on purpose, with both of you choosing it. Clear rulings on cheek kisses, pecks, dares, stage kisses and kisses you never wanted.

The Short Answer

A first kiss is the first time your lips meet someone else's lips on purpose, with both of you choosing it. By that test a kiss on the cheek doesn't count, a quick peck on the lips does, and a kiss someone took without your yes never has to. Dares, party games and stage kisses sit in a gray zone, and there you get the deciding vote.

Somebody asks, "When was your first kiss?" and a small committee convenes in your head.

There was the peck on a dare at a party, over in two seconds while six people counted out loud. There was the date who kissed your cheek at the door and left you on the step wondering if that was the beginning of something. Maybe there was a kiss you never wanted. Maybe there's nothing yet, and you're trying to work out whether that one dare quietly cancelled your "never been kissed" status.

Nobody hands out a rulebook for this, which is exactly why you're searching what counts as a first kiss. Fine. I'll write one.

Here's the short version: a first kiss is the first time your lips meet someone else's lips on purpose, with both of you choosing it. A kiss on the cheek doesn't count. A quick peck on the lips does. A kiss someone took without your yes never has to. Everything else lives in a gray zone, and in the gray zone you get the deciding vote.

What counts as a first kiss

A kiss, by the plain dictionary definition, is touching someone with your lips, whether out of love, affection or simple hello. By that standard your aunt has been kissing you since before you could walk, and nobody counts her.

A first kiss means something narrower. When people ask about yours, they mean the first romantic one. Three things have to be true:

  • Lips on lips. Not the cheek, not the forehead, not the back of the hand. Mouth to mouth, even for a second. There's a reason the line sits right there: your lips are some of the most touch-sensitive skin you have, and a kiss on them lands differently than a kiss anywhere else on your face.
  • On purpose. A collision doesn't count. Two heads turning the same way during a hug is an accident, not a milestone.
  • Both of you chose it. One person leaning in isn't enough. The other person has to lean back, or at the very least welcome it.

That third condition is the one people forget, and it matters most. In Hitch, Will Smith's date doctor tells his client to go 90 percent of the way and then wait for the other person to come the last 10. That last 10 percent is the difference between a first kiss and a thing that happened to someone's face. (It's also why fairy tales age so badly. By this test, a surprising number of sleeping princesses are still waiting on their first kiss.)

Notice what isn't on the list. Tongue isn't. Length isn't. Neither is quality, fireworks, a soundtrack or a sunset. A one-second peck behind a bowling alley qualifies. A disaster qualifies too; plenty of real first kisses are genuinely bad, and they still count.

Does a kiss on the cheek count as a first kiss?

No. A kiss on the cheek is a kiss. It just isn't a first kiss.

The cheek is shared territory. Across much of Europe, Latin America and the Middle East, cheek kissing is how people say hello to friends and family. If cheek kisses counted, a large share of the planet would have had their first kiss at a family dinner, somewhere between the bread basket and the goodbyes.

A romantic cheek kiss is a different animal. A date who kisses your cheek at the door and lingers there is telling you something, and a kiss on the cheek can mean anything from "I like you" to "I like you as a friend." But even the most loaded cheek kiss is a question, not the answer. It's the trailer, not the movie.

The same ruling covers the forehead, the hand, the top of the head and the air kiss beside your ear. Real kisses, every one. Not your first.

What about a kiss that lands on the corner of the mouth? Half lip, half cheek, and a genuinely close call. If it was aimed there as an invitation and neither of you took it further, I'd call it the moment before your first kiss. If it was the start of a real kiss that simply landed off-center, count it.

Does a peck on the lips count?

Yes. A peck counts.

A peck on the lips is a quick, closed-mouth kiss, and it meets all three conditions: lips on lips, on purpose, both of you in. It doesn't matter that it lasted one second. It doesn't matter that nobody's knees buckled.

People discount pecks because they're short and they didn't feel like the movies. The movies are edited. Real first kisses are usually small, a little clumsy and over before your brain finishes processing them. There's no minimum duration for a first kiss; how long a kiss should last is about what feels good, not what qualifies.

A first kiss doesn't need tongue, either. Most of them shouldn't have any. Tongue on a first kiss is its own question, and the answer there is "not right away."

The gray zone: dares, games and stage kisses

This is where the real confusion lives. These kisses pass the lips test, but the "both of you chose it" part is fuzzy. Here are my rulings.

A kiss on a dare or in a party game

Spin the bottle. Truth or dare. Seven minutes in a closet with a near stranger and somebody's winter coats. You agreed to play, so in the strictest sense you chose it. But you chose the game, not the person, and usually not the moment.

My ruling: it counts if you want it to. Plenty of people happily say "my first kiss was spin the bottle" and laugh about it. Plenty of others file it under party trick and count the first kiss they actually wanted. Both answers are honest.

A stage or screen kiss

Actors kiss for work: in plays, student films and acting classes. It's lips on lips, on purpose, and both people agreed. But what they agreed to was a scene. Stage kisses are usually rehearsed and blocked like any other piece of choreography, and the two people in it were playing someone else.

My ruling: a stage kiss doesn't have to count. If you'd rather save "first kiss" for a moment that belonged to you instead of your character, that's a completely fair call.

The kiss you didn't kiss back

Someone leaned in, their lips landed on yours, and you froze. You didn't pull away, but you didn't really take part either. It was over before you'd decided anything.

My ruling: this one is yours to call. If you welcomed it and simply didn't know what to do with your mouth yet (extremely normal), count it. If you froze because you didn't want it, read the next section, because the answer there is different.

The accidental bump

Two cheek kisses aimed the same way, a hug that turned into a head-on collision, a lurch on a crowded train. Doesn't count. Intent is a requirement, and gravity is not a romantic partner.

The tiebreaker: the Technically Test

When you're stuck, tell the story out loud.

If the honest version needs the word technically, you're allowed not to count it.

"Well, technically my first kiss was a dare at a party." Technically is the sound a story makes when it doesn't feel like yours. Nobody keeps an official register of first kisses, and there's no referee. You're the historian of your own life, and historians get to decide which event goes in the first chapter.

A kiss you didn't want doesn't have to be your first kiss

This part gets a straight answer, no jokes.

If someone kissed you without your yes (you said no, you pulled away, you were too drunk or too frightened to respond, or you simply didn't want it), that was not your first kiss. That was something that happened to you. A first kiss is something you do with someone.

You don't owe that moment the title. You're allowed to say "I haven't had my first kiss yet" and mean every word. You're allowed to keep that name for a kiss you choose.

If the memory still sits heavily, it's worth talking to someone you trust. In the US, RAINN's National Sexual Assault Hotline is free, confidential and open around the clock at 800-656-4673.

Kisses that never count (and aren't wasted)

A few things never count as a first kiss, and every one of them is perfectly fine:

  • Practice. Your hand, your pillow, a peach, the bathroom mirror. Not a first kiss, and nothing to be embarrassed about either. Practicing kissing by yourself can take real edge off the nerves.
  • Family. A peck from a parent or grandparent is love, not a first kiss, even when it lands on your lips.
  • Blown kisses, air kisses and kiss emojis. Warm, but they never touched you. A text ending in a kiss emoji is not a first kiss, no matter how long you stared at it.
  • The one you only imagined. You know the one. The kiss you ran through four hundred times on the drive home. That's rehearsal, not history.

Can you still say you've never been kissed?

If your only kisses are gray-zone ones, yes, you can say it honestly. When people ask about romantic experience, a dare at a party isn't what they're asking about.

If you'd like to be precise, there's a graceful way to put it: "I've never had a real first kiss. There was a dare once, but I don't count it." It's true, it's light, and it spares you a long explanation.

You're in better company than you think. In Never Been Kissed, Drew Barrymore plays a 25-year-old copy editor who ends the film standing in the middle of a baseball field with a countdown timer running, waiting for a kiss that counts. The whole movie is an argument for holding out for one you actually want.

If you're further along the timeline than your friends, the average age of a first kiss is far less of a deadline than it looks. And whether to tell someone it's your first is a real question with a kind answer, which I cover in the never-been-kissed guide.

When a couple can't agree on their first kiss

"First kiss" has a second meaning: the first kiss between two particular people. Couples argue about this one too.

One of you counts the clumsy kiss at a friend's party months before you got together. The other counts the doorstep kiss after your first real date, the one that felt like a decision. You're both right, and you don't need a judge.

The three-part test still helps. The kiss where you both clearly chose each other is the one that anchors the story. The earlier one keeps a title of its own. Call the party kiss the pilot episode and the doorstep kiss the premiere.

It's a lovely argument to have. Both sides are bragging about the same person.

How to make the next one count

If your real first kiss hasn't happened yet, you don't need a perfect setting. You need the three things you now know by heart: lips, intent and a yes.

  • Get the yes. Watch for them leaning in too, or ask outright. Asking for a kiss sounds scarier than it is, and a clear yes makes the moment easier for both of you.
  • Keep it short. A few seconds, closed lips, relaxed mouth. Kissing someone for the first time is mostly about staying present, not performing.
  • Let it be a little awkward. Noses bump. Someone laughs. That's a first kiss doing its job.

The bottom line

A first kiss is the first time your lips meet someone else's, on purpose, with both of you choosing it. The cheek kiss is a kiss but not your first. The peck counts. The dare counts if you want it to. The kiss you didn't choose never has to.

And if some strange, half-counted moment from years ago is still bothering you, remember who's holding the pen. Your first kiss is the one you can tell without the word "technically."

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as a first kiss?

A first kiss is the first time your lips touch someone else's lips on purpose, with both of you choosing it. It can be a one-second peck or a long kiss, with or without tongue. Kisses on the cheek, forehead or hand don't count, and neither does an accidental bump or a kiss someone took without your consent.

Does a kiss on the cheek count as a first kiss?

No. A kiss on the cheek is a real kiss, but it isn't a first kiss, because a first kiss means lips on lips. Cheek kisses are an everyday greeting in much of the world, so they don't mark the romantic milestone people mean when they ask about your first kiss.

Does a kiss on the cheek count as a kiss?

Yes. Touching someone with your lips as a sign of affection or greeting is a kiss. It just isn't a first kiss, which people reserve for the first romantic kiss on the lips.

Does a peck count as a first kiss?

Yes. A peck is a quick, closed-mouth kiss on the lips, and if it was on purpose and you both wanted it, it counts. There's no minimum length and no tongue requirement.

Does a first kiss have to have tongue?

No. Most first kisses are closed-mouth, and many of the best ones are. Tongue is something to add later, once you've both settled in, and it has nothing to do with whether a kiss counts.

Does a kiss on a dare count as your first kiss?

It counts if you want it to. You agreed to the game, so you can count it, but you didn't necessarily choose the person or the moment, so you can also set it aside and count the first kiss you actually wanted. Both answers are honest.

Does it count as a first kiss if you didn't kiss back?

If you wanted the kiss and just froze, you can count it. If you didn't want it, it doesn't have to count. A first kiss is something you do with someone, not something that happens to you.

Does a kiss you didn't want count as your first kiss?

No, unless you decide otherwise. A kiss someone took without your yes isn't a first kiss in any meaningful sense, and you're free to keep that title for one you choose. If the memory still weighs on you, talking to someone you trust can help; in the US, RAINN's hotline is available around the clock at 800-656-4673.

Does a stage kiss count as a first kiss?

It doesn't have to. A stage or screen kiss is agreed to as part of a scene, so you're free not to count it as your personal first kiss. If it felt like yours, count it; if it felt like your character's, don't.

How long does a kiss have to be to count as a first kiss?

There's no minimum. A one-second peck counts as much as a long kiss, as long as it was lips on lips, on purpose and mutual.
