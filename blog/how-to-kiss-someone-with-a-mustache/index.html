How to Kiss Someone With a Mustache (From Stubble to Handlebar)

How to kiss someone with a mustache at every stage, from scratchy week-one stubble to a waxed handlebar, plus the tickle fix, the rash fix and what to say.

The Short Answer

Kiss their lower lip, the one with no hair on it, tilt your head a little more than usual and use steady, slightly firmer pressure instead of light brushing. Light, moving contact is the kind of touch that tickles, and fast, sliding kisses are what scratch, so slow down too. The person with the mustache can help by keeping it clean, trimming any hairs that hang over the upper lip and approaching from slightly above. The first few days of new growth scratch the most; the hairs ease up once they're long enough to bend.

November has a side effect nobody prints on the poster.

All month, perfectly nice upper lips quietly turn into hedges. Some are growing for Movember. Some are an experiment. Some belong to people who watched Top Gun: Maverick once and never fully recovered. Whatever the reason, the next kiss is going to feel different, and you'd like to know what to do about it before your top lip finds out the hard way.

Let me be direct. Here's how to kiss someone with a mustache: aim for their lower lip, the one without hair on it, tilt your head a little more than usual, and trade light brushing for steady, slightly firmer pressure. Light, moving contact is exactly the kind of touch that tickles, and fast, sliding kisses are what turn stubble into a rash. The person wearing the mustache has homework too: keep it clean, keep it off the lip and come in from slightly above. Below is the whole playbook, stage by stage and style by style, plus what to say when the mustache is winning.

Why a Mustache Feels Nothing Like a Beard

A beard frames a kiss. A mustache is in it.

Think about the geography. A beard lives on the jaw, chin and cheeks, which meet you around the edges of a kiss. A mustache lives on the strip of skin directly above the mouth, so it touches your lips on every single kiss, quick pecks included. And it lands on some of the most touch-hungry skin you own (here's why your lips feel everything).

When someone says a mustache is "a lot," they usually mean one of three things:

  • The poke. Short, freshly cut hairs with blunt tips that push into your lip instead of bending away from it.
  • The tickle. Longer hairs skating lightly across your skin. A completely different sensation, with a completely different fix.
  • The extras. Wax, coffee foam, crumbs and anything else the mustache has collected since breakfast.

If your person has a full beard as well, my guide to how to kiss someone with a beard handles the jaw, cheeks and chin. This one stays on the small patch above the lip, because that's where the kiss actually happens.

How to Kiss Someone With a Mustache in 6 Steps

These work on every mustache, from day-three fuzz to a full Tom Selleck. The first four do most of the heavy lifting.

1. Aim for the lower lip

Their lower lip is the bare one, and it happens to be the easiest lip to kiss well. Take it gently between yours, hold for a beat, then let go. That catch, hold and release is the core move in how to move your lips when kissing, and it could have been designed for this exact situation.

Your top lip will still meet the mustache. That's fine. Your attention, and most of your pressure, stays below it.

2. Tilt a little more than usual

A slightly deeper angle changes which part of the mustache meets your mouth. Every mustache has a gentler patch and a scratchier one, and tilting is how you find out which is which. If you're never sure which way to lean in the first place, which way to tilt your head when kissing settles it.

3. Commit to the pressure

Here's the counterintuitive one. Facing a bristly upper lip, your instinct is to kiss as lightly as humanly possible. Against a mustache, that backfires.

A very light movement across the skin produces that crawly, itchy kind of tickle; it even has a name, knismesis, sometimes described as a moving itch. Featherlight kisses deliver exactly that. Steady, definite pressure gives the hairs far less room to skate around. Not crushing. Just present.

4. Slow down

Speed is friction. Quick, sliding kisses drag short bristles back and forth across your lip like a scrub brush across a pan. Slow kisses barely slide at all. The slow kiss explains why slowing down improves nearly every kiss; with a mustache involved, it's also self-defense.

5. Steer with your hands

A hand along their jaw or cheek lets you set the angle without a negotiation. If one spot scratches, nudge their face a few degrees and carry on. Nobody has to say a word.

6. Laugh, then say something

If it tickles, laugh. Laughing mid-kiss isn't a failure; it means you're comfortable with each other. Then use an actual sentence. "Your mustache is winning" is a perfectly good one, and it opens the door to fixing it together. There are more scripts below.

What Kissing a Mustache Feels Like, Week by Week

Movember takes its name from Australian slang (a mustache is a "mo") and has been raising money for men's health since 2003, when it started in Melbourne. It also runs an unofficial experiment on everyone who kisses a participant: thirty days of an upper lip that gets a little hairier every morning.

Here's how that month feels from the receiving end. Facial hair grows at different speeds on different faces, so treat these as phases, not deadlines.

Days 1 to 4: The Velcro phase

Here's what nobody tells you: the first week is the worst week. Every hair has just been cut straight across, and it's too short to bend, so it pushes into your lip like the hook side of Velcro. Friction burn happens fastest here.

Keep kisses short and still, lean hard on step one, and save the long make-outs for later in the month. A thin layer of lip balm before you kiss buys you a little cushion.

Week 2: The awkward middle

The hairs are long enough to flex a little and still short enough to prickle. Because longer hairs can bend instead of stab, this is usually where the scratch starts to ease. The first tickles show up around now too.

Weeks 3 and 4: The tickle era

The hair lies down. The scratch mostly fades and the tickle takes over, which makes step three your best friend. This is also when stray hairs start creeping over the edge of the lip and into the kiss, and that's the cue for a trim.

Month two and beyond: Styled territory

If the mustache survives into December, it gets a personality: wax, shape, maybe curled tips. That's a different negotiation, and it's the next section.

The Velcro phase ends. Hold on to that.

How to Kiss Every Mustache Style

Different mustaches park hair in different places, so each one has its own best approach.

  • The pencil (think Clark Gable in Gone with the Wind). A thin, precisely edged line. Very little hair, but it's usually trimmed often, so the edge can feel like a fine line of grit. Stay on the lower lip and keep the sliding to a minimum.
  • The chevron (Tom Selleck in Magnum, P.I., or Freddie Mercury). Thick and wide, covering most of the upper lip. Expect a dense brush across your top lip and plenty of tickle. Firm, steady pressure is your friend, and a trim along the lip line is a fair request.
  • The horseshoe (Hulk Hogan). Runs past the corners of the mouth and down toward the jaw. The hair meets the corners of your mouth and your chin, not just your top lip, so a deep tilt steers you straight into it. Keep the kiss more central.
  • The handlebar (Hercule Poirot's waxed points). Waxed and curled at the tips, which stick out to the sides. This is the one style where a straighter approach beats an extra tilt, unless you enjoy a curl in the cheek. The wax brings its own issues, covered below.
  • The walrus. The big, droopy one that hangs over the whole mouth. It's the style most likely to put hair in your mouth during open-mouth kisses. Focus on the lower lip, and if it keeps happening, ask for a lip-line trim.

A neat pencil can scratch more than a shaggy walrus. Length isn't the enemy. Short is.

The Mustache Problems Nobody Warns You About

Beyond scratch and tickle, mustaches come with a few side quests.

  • Foam and crumbs. A mustache catches whatever passes beneath it. A cappuccino-foam mustache is adorable for about one second. Wearers: napkin after every drink, and a quick check in your phone camera before the goodnight kiss.
  • Wax. Wax holds a shape, and it also transfers. A little on the sides and tips does the job; the middle strip that touches lips can usually go without. Too much, and every kiss tastes like the inside of the tin.
  • Lipstick. Bold lipstick plus a light-colored mustache equals a crime scene. If you wear color, kissing with lipstick on covers the formulas and tricks that leave less evidence behind.
  • Hair in your mouth. Usually a lip-line problem, occasionally a direction problem. Brushing the mustache outward toward the corners of the mouth trains it to part away from the kiss.
  • French kissing. Completely doable. Keep the open-mouth part slow, keep your lips relaxed and stay focused on the lower lip, and the mustache mostly stays outside the action. How to French kiss covers everything else.

Mustache Rash: What It Is and When It's Something Else

That pink, tender patch on and above your top lip after a long kiss is friction irritation: the same thing as beard burn, concentrated into a smaller area. It usually fades within a day or two.

  • Right after: rinse with cool water, pat dry and smooth on a fragrance-free moisturizer.
  • For the next day or two: skip exfoliants and retinol on that spot, and give it a break from marathon sessions.
  • Next time: a thin layer of lip balm or petroleum jelly on and around your lips before a long kiss cuts down the friction.

The beard guide's section on dealing with beard burn walks through the full aftercare.

Know what isn't rash, too. Rash is redness and tenderness. Sores or blisters that burst and leave crusty golden-brown patches look more like impetigo, a skin infection the NHS says spreads easily through skin-to-skin contact. A blister at the edge of the lip that started with tingling, itching or burning is more likely a cold sore, and the NHS is clear that you shouldn't kiss anyone while you have one. Neither is a mustache problem. Both are a reason to stop kissing and see a pharmacist or doctor.

If You're the One With the Mustache

Your partner can do everything above and still lose to a neglected mo. Your half of the deal is short, and none of it requires a new personality. (For the full softening routine, how to make your beard soft for kissing covers mustaches too.)

  • Do the mo check. Before any kiss you can see coming: foam, crumbs, strays, too much wax. Two seconds.
  • Keep it off the lip. Hairs hanging over the edge of your upper lip are the ones that end up in someone's mouth. Trim them with your mouth relaxed.
  • Come in from slightly above. Approaching from a touch higher lets the mustache rest over their top lip instead of driving into it. If you're the taller one, this happens on its own; if you're not, the moves in kissing with a height difference apply here too.
  • Lead with your lower lip. It's your hairless asset. Offer it first.
  • Let them set the pace for the first week. A new mustache is new for both of you. Follow their speed until you know how it lands.
  • Ask once, then listen. "Is the mustache working for you?" is a generous question. A long pause is an answer.

What to Say When the Mustache Is Winning

Most mustache problems survive because nobody wants to insult a face. Keep it about the hair, keep it light and attach a fix:

  • "I love the mustache. My top lip would like to file a complaint."
  • "Could you trim the bit over your lip? I keep getting a mouthful."
  • "Slower? It scratches when we speed up."
  • "Kiss me from up here," and then show them.

If you'd rather say nothing at all, steps one, two and five redirect a kiss silently. For trickier conversations about kissing in general, how to tell someone they're a bad kisser has scripts that keep feelings intact.

And if you simply don't like kissing it? You're allowed. It's their face, so the mustache is their call. It's your mouth, so how much you kiss it is yours. There's usually a friendly middle, like a shorter trim on date nights.

The Bottom Line

Kissing someone with a mustache comes down to geography and speed: favor the bare lower lip, tilt a little more, press like you mean it and slow everything down. The person wearing it keeps it clean, keeps it off the lip and comes in from above.

Do that and the mustache stops being an obstacle and becomes texture. You might even miss it in December.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you kiss someone with a mustache?

Focus on their lower lip, which has no hair on it, tilt your head slightly more than usual and use steady, slightly firmer pressure instead of light brushing. Keep the kiss slow, since fast, sliding kisses cause most of the scratching. The person with the mustache can help by trimming hairs off the lip line and approaching from slightly above.

Does kissing someone with a mustache tickle?

Often, especially once the hair is a few weeks long. Light, moving contact across the skin is what sets off that itchy tickle, so steadier pressure and less sliding usually calm it down. Short stubble tends to scratch rather than tickle.

Is a mustache or a beard worse for kissing?

For the kiss itself, the mustache usually matters more, because it sits on the upper lip and touches your mouth on every kiss. A beard mostly meets your chin and cheeks. Length matters more than style: a short, freshly trimmed mustache scratches more than a longer one.

Why does a short mustache scratch more than a long one?

Freshly cut hairs are short and blunt, so they can't bend, and they push straight into the skin. Once the hairs are long enough to flex, they bend away on contact, and the scratch turns into a tickle.

Can you French kiss someone with a mustache?

Yes. Keep the open-mouth kissing slow, keep your lips relaxed and focus on their lower lip so the mustache stays mostly outside the kiss. A trim along the lip line helps if hair keeps ending up in your mouth.

How do you treat mustache burn on your lip?

Rinse with cool water, pat dry and apply a fragrance-free moisturizer. Skip exfoliants and retinol on the area until it settles, which usually takes a day or two. If you see blisters, sores or crusty golden patches, that isn't friction burn; stop kissing and check with a pharmacist or doctor.

Do people like kissing someone with a mustache?

It's personal. Some people love the texture, some tolerate it for the person attached to it, and some would rather not. The only opinion that counts is your partner's, so ask.
