What Does a Kiss on the Nose Mean? (Plus the Nose Rub)

A kiss on the nose usually means you're adored, not seduced. What it says from a partner, date or friend, how to answer it, and the real story of the nose rub.

The Short Answer

A kiss on the nose usually means affection without pressure: they find you adorable, feel safe with you, and aren't trying to turn the moment into anything more. It is playful and protective rather than seductive, so read it as comfort, not heat. From a partner it's everyday fondness, from a new date it's warm interest that isn't in a hurry, and a nose rub (what many people still call an Eskimo kiss) carries the same tender message.

Nobody plans a kiss on the nose.

It happens in the gaps. You are halfway through a very serious argument about whose turn it was to buy coffee, you are winning, and then someone leans over and plants one right on the tip of your nose. Argument over. You lost. You are also, somehow, grinning.

That tiny ambush is exactly why people type what does a kiss on the nose mean into a search bar. It isn't romantic the way a kiss on the lips is, it isn't polite the way a peck on the cheek is, and it lands on the one feature of your face that almost never gets poetry written about it.

The good news: it is one of the easiest kisses to read. The better news: the nose rub, the gesture a lot of us grew up calling an Eskimo kiss, has a real history that beats the cartoon version. Here is what both mean and what to do when one lands on you.

Why the Nose Is Such a Specific Place to Kiss

Think about the geography. Your nose sits an inch above your mouth. Anyone close enough to kiss your nose was close enough to kiss your lips, and they chose not to. That choice is the whole message.

A kiss on the lips asks a question. A kiss on the nose answers one: I like you, I'm comfortable with you, and right now I'm not trying to turn this into anything. It is affection with the pressure taken out.

It is also strictly face to face, close enough to see their eyes crinkle. That closeness, with the total absence of heat, is why it reads as tender rather than flirty. A kiss on the cheek can be pure etiquette between acquaintances. Nobody kisses an acquaintance on the nose.

Its closest relatives are the forehead kiss and the kiss on top of the head. All three skip the mouth on purpose and say more about trust than desire. The nose kiss is simply the one with a sense of humor.

The Five Things a Kiss on the Nose Usually Says

One gesture, a handful of meanings. Most nose kisses land in one of these, and plenty hit two at once.

You're Adorable to Me

The classic. You did something ridiculous and charming: sneezed like a cartoon mouse, got flour on your face, lost an argument with a parking machine. The nose kiss is the physical version of saying you are too cute to deal with. Nobody kisses the nose of a person who irritates them.

I've Got You

The protective version is quieter. It shows up when you are tired, overwhelmed or a little fragile, often with a hand on your cheek. It says: you are safe here, and nothing is being asked of you. A forehead kiss says the same thing more solemnly. The nose kiss keeps it light.

I'm Not in a Hurry

On a date, a nose kiss often means someone wants closeness without rushing the next step. They're savoring the part before.

Relax, We're Okay

The nose kiss is the world's best tension breaker. Mid-sulk or mid-spiral, a quick kiss on the nose resets the room. It says I'm still on your side, even while we disagree about the dishwasher. I have been disarmed this way mid-rant more than once, and I'm still not convinced it was a fair fight. During a conversation that genuinely matters to you, though, it can feel like being patted on the head.

Wait for It

Sometimes the nose kiss is a detour on the way somewhere better. Lips hover, drift up to the tip of your nose, then come back down to your mouth a second later. That is a tease, and a good one: it stretches the moment so the real kiss lands harder, which is the whole art of teasing with a kiss.

What a Nose Kiss Means Depends on Who Gave It

The same kiss changes meaning with the relationship. A rough guide:

  • From a partner: fondness, almost always. A small, unperformed gesture that says affection is woven into the ordinary day. If it arrives out of nowhere with nothing wanted in return, that is a very good sign.
  • From someone you're dating: warm interest with the brakes lightly on. It is flirtier than a hug and less committal than a kiss on the lips. What happens next tells you which way it leans.
  • From a friend: platonic affection, somewhere between a hug and a hair ruffle. With lingering eye contact and a pause, that friend may be checking whether you'd welcome more.
  • From a parent or grandparent: pure family love. Plenty of us collected nose kisses as kids, which is part of why the gesture still reads as safe when we are grown.

Context beats rules. A nose kiss after an evening of flirting is not the same kiss as one at a family barbecue, and you already know which one you got.

Kiss on the Nose vs. Nose Rub: Not the Same Gesture

People use the phrase nose kiss for two different things, which causes half the confusion.

A kiss on the nose is lips on the tip of someone's nose. Quick, light, often a little silly.

A nose rub is nose against nose: two people graze the tips of their noses together, no lips involved. It is the gesture many people grew up calling an Eskimo kiss, and today it is just as often called a nose kiss or a nose rub.

Both are affectionate and low-pressure. The nose rub tends to be slower and more intimate, because you are sharing the same few inches of air. The butterfly kiss, eyelashes fluttered against a cheek, is a different creature entirely; it lives with the other oddities in my guide to the types of kisses and what they mean.

Where the Name Eskimo Kiss Comes From (and Why Many Avoid It)

The name is usually traced to an Inuit custom called the kunik, and the kunik is not two noses wagging back and forth. You press your nose against someone's cheek and breathe in. NPR's reporting on Inuit parenting describes it as a special kiss for babies and young children: nose against the cheek, then a sniff of the skin. If you have ever buried your face in a baby's cheek and breathed in, you already understand it better than any cartoon does.

Then there is the word itself. In 2010 the Inuit Circumpolar Council, which represents Inuit across four countries, passed a resolution on terminology stating that Eskimo "is not an Inuit term" and calling on researchers and others to use Inuit instead. The Alaska Native Language Center at the University of Alaska Fairbanks says the name is now considered unacceptable by many or even most Alaska Natives, largely because it is a colonial name imposed by outsiders, and that many prefer names from their own languages, such as Inupiaq or Yupik.

Usage is not uniform. NPR's explainer on the word notes that people in many parts of the Arctic consider it derogatory because of how colonizers used it, while many Alaska Natives still use it, partly because Inuit is not a word in the Yupik languages. Its advice for outsiders: you probably shouldn't use it.

What should you call it, then? For noses together, nose rub or nose kiss is clear and nobody winces. If you mean the Inuit custom itself, the word is kunik, and it belongs to the families who practice it. I use the older phrase here only because people still search for it.

Other Cultures That Greet Nose First

Other cultures put the nose to work too, as a greeting or a mark of respect rather than a seduction.

In Aotearoa New Zealand, the Māori hongi is the ceremonial pressing of noses that comes at the end of a formal welcome, confirming that two groups are meeting in peace. According to Te Ara, the Encyclopedia of New Zealand, the custom refers to Tāne Mahuta, who shaped a woman from earth and breathed life into her through the nose.

In parts of the Gulf and Yemen, touching noses is a traditional greeting too. A writer for Al Arabiya called it a handshake by other means, and explained that when two people are not peers, the younger or junior one kisses the other's nose as a mark of respect.

Notice the pattern. In all three traditions, nose contact is about peace, respect or family, not seduction. It says I see you and I mean you no harm, which is a fine summary of what a kiss on the tip of your nose says too.

How to Answer a Kiss on the Nose

Someone just kissed your nose. You have about a second and a half. The good options:

  • Scrunch and laugh. A crinkled nose and a grin tells them it landed.
  • Return the favor. Kiss their nose right back, or graze the tip of your nose against theirs. Either way it becomes a tiny game.
  • Answer with your lips. If the mood is warm and you have been wanting to, meet it with a real kiss. Plenty of nose kisses are open invitations, and kissing back is mostly a matter of matching their pace.
  • Ask about it. A smiling what was that for? often gets a sweeter answer than you expected.

And if it felt patronizing, like a pat on the head in the middle of something serious? Say so, kindly: I know you mean it sweetly, but right now I need you to hear me. A nose kiss is meant to lower the temperature, and a partner who cares will adjust.

Is a Nose Kiss a Signal to Slow Down or Speed Up?

Nose kisses often work as a dial rather than a destination.

It usually means slow down when:

  • it replaced a lip kiss you were both clearly leaning toward;
  • it came with a step back, a quick hug or a change of subject;
  • it was followed by little eye contact and a brisk goodnight.

That is someone keeping things warm but unhurried: nerves, pacing, or a soft no to anything more tonight. Treat it as a yellow light and don't push past it.

It usually means keep going when:

  • their face stays close after the kiss instead of pulling away;
  • their eyes drop to your mouth;
  • they are smiling, a little breathless, and going nowhere.

That is a nose kiss as an overture. The cues overlap heavily with the signs someone wants to kiss you, and when you see them, a slow move toward their lips is rarely a misread. Can't tell? Ask. Can I kiss you properly? has never ruined a moment that a nose kiss started.

How to Give a Nose Kiss That Lands

On the giving end, this is one of the most underused kisses you own.

  • Pick a light moment. Nose kisses shine when someone is being ridiculous, sleepy or flustered, and fall flat on a partner who is upset and wants to be heard.
  • Keep it quick and aim for the tip. A light tap. Halfway up the bridge feels like you missed.
  • Stay close for a beat. Pull back an inch, not a foot, and look at them. That pause is where the affection registers.
  • Use it as an ambush. A nose kiss out of nowhere, while they are reading or cooking, is exactly the kind of surprise kiss that sticks in someone's head all day.

For a nose rub, go slower: faces close, tips grazing, a breath or two. And if your real worry is where your nose goes during an ordinary lip kiss, that is a geometry question with its own answer in what to do with your nose when kissing.

The Bottom Line

A kiss on the nose means affection without an agenda. From a partner it is fondness, from a date a warm but unhurried maybe, and from friends or family it is trust. The nose rub is its slower cousin, and the old name for it is usually traced to the kunik, which is really a nose-to-cheek sniff.

Whichever one you got, the message underneath is the same: I see you, I'm comfortable with you, and I mean you no harm.

Scrunch your face. Laugh. Kiss them back if you want to.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean when someone kisses your nose?

It usually means affection without pressure: they find you adorable, feel comfortable with you, and are not trying to escalate. Who gave it and what happened right after tell you the rest.

Is a kiss on the nose romantic?

It can be, but it is more tender than steamy. Between partners it signals fondness, and on a date it can be a prelude to a real kiss, especially when their face stays close and their eyes drop to your lips.

What does it mean when a guy kisses your nose?

The same thing it means from anyone: he finds you cute and feels close to you. From a partner it is everyday fondness; from a new date, warm interest that is not in a rush.

What is a nose kiss called?

A kiss on the tip of the nose is simply a nose kiss. Rubbing noses is a nose rub, long called an Eskimo kiss; the Inuit nose-to-cheek sniff is the kunik, and the Māori greeting of pressing noses is the hongi.

What is an Eskimo kiss?

It is the popular name for two people rubbing the tips of their noses together. The Inuit kunik it is usually traced to is actually a nose pressed to the cheek and a breath in, and because many Inuit and Alaska Natives reject the word Eskimo, nose rub is the better name today.

Does a nose kiss mean someone wants to slow things down?

Sometimes. If it replaced a lip kiss you were both leaning toward and came with a step back, read it as a request to keep things unhurried. If they stayed close and kept smiling, it was more likely an invitation.

How should you respond to a kiss on the nose?

Laugh, scrunch your nose, or kiss theirs right back. If the mood is warm, answer with a kiss on the lips; if it felt dismissive during a serious moment, tell them kindly that you need them to listen instead.
