What Does a Kiss on the Palm Mean? (And on the Wrist)

A kiss on the palm is the private side of a hand kiss: desire, devotion or a kiss to keep. How to read one, what a wrist kiss adds, and how to answer it.

The Short Answer

A kiss on the palm usually means desire or devotion from someone who already feels close to you. The back of the hand is the polite, public side of a hand kiss; turning your hand over to kiss the palm is a private choice. It can also be a kiss to keep (especially if they fold your fingers over it), a wordless I'm here, or a bold flirt. A kiss on the inside of the wrist, right over your pulse, usually means longing: they're taking the slow road on purpose.

Here's the short version: a kiss on the palm usually means desire, devotion or both, from someone who already feels close to you. The back of the hand is the public side of a hand kiss, where manners live. The palm is the side you have to be let into. When someone turns your hand over to kiss it, the turn is half the message.

The Austrian poet Franz Grillparzer said as much in the nineteenth century. His poem Kuss is eight lines long and gives a meaning to every place a kiss can land. Respect kisses the hands. Friendship takes the forehead, fondness the cheek, love the mouth. And in die hohle Hand, into the hollow of the hand, goes desire.

Same hand. Other side. Completely different sentence.

What a kiss on the palm means

Start with the etiquette, because the palm kiss is defined by breaking it. In the formal European hand kiss, a lady offers her hand with the back facing up, and in formal settings today the lips often don't touch skin at all. The whole ritual is built around the back of the hand. Nobody offers a stranger their palm.

A palm kiss skips the ceremony. It says I'm not greeting you. I'm closer than that.

Your skin agrees. The palm is glabrous skin: hairless, rich in touch receptors, and the same kind of skin you'll find on your fingertips and your lips. The back of your hand is ordinary hair-bearing skin, like your forearm. A kiss on the knuckles lands on the outside of you. A kiss on the palm lands on a surface built to notice detail, which is one reason it registers so much more. (For the full wiring diagram, why lips are so sensitive walks through it.)

Then there's what a palm is for. It's where you receive things: keys, change, a note slipped to you under a table. A kiss placed there is a kiss handed to you. That's why so many palm kisses feel less like a gesture and more like a gift.

What it almost never means is "nice to meet you." If a kiss on the back of the hand is a bow, a kiss on the palm is a confession. For the public version and its seven moods, what a hand kiss means covers the whole family. This page is about the private side.

Five things a kiss on the palm can mean

The turn tells you it's personal. The rest of the moment tells you which kind of personal.

1. Desire: "I want more of you"

This is Grillparzer's reading, and it's the one most people are quietly hoping for.

It's slow. Their lips stay a beat longer than a gesture needs. Their eyes come up to yours during the kiss or right after, and they don't let go of your hand. Very often the kiss travels: palm, then the inside of your wrist, then an inch or two up your forearm, each stop a little slower than the last.

That trail is a tease in the best sense. They're kissing everything except your mouth, and they know you've noticed. It's the same logic as the kiss migration in how to tease with a kiss: take the long way and let the other person feel every mile of it.

If this one happens on a couch at the end of a good night, it's rarely the last kiss of the evening.

2. Devotion: "I'd kneel if I could"

Edith Wharton wrote the definitive version in The Age of Innocence. Newland Archer is riding in a carriage onto a ferry beside Ellen Olenska when he "unbuttoned her tight brown glove, and kissed her palm as if he had kissed a relic."

A relic. Not a hand; a sacred object.

That's the devotion kiss. It's still rather than slow. Their eyes are often closed, and both of their hands cradle yours as if it might break. It shows up at big moments: a proposal, a hospital waiting room, the end of a terrible week, the first quiet minute after a fight when neither of you has the right words yet.

A forehead kiss says I'll look after you. A devotion palm kiss says something closer to I can't believe I get you.

(Archer, for the record, was married at the time, and not to Ellen. Whether a kiss like that counts as cheating is a messier question than it looks.)

3. The keepsake: "Hold on to this"

They kiss the middle of your palm, then fold your fingers closed over it.

This one has a famous origin story. In Audrey Penn's 1993 picture book The Kissing Hand, a mother raccoon kisses the center of her son Chester's palm and tells him to press that hand to his cheek whenever he feels lonely at school. Three decades of nervous kids have walked into classrooms holding an invisible kiss.

It isn't only for kids. In 2004 the U.S. Army bought 14,000 copies for soldiers stationed abroad to share with their families.

Between adults, the keepsake kiss lives at departure gates, open car windows and front doors at 6 a.m. before a long trip. It means I'll miss you, and here's something to carry until I'm back. It's a goodbye kiss you get to keep.

4. Comfort: "I'm right here"

Sometimes they take your hand, press your palm flat against their own cheek, and turn their face to kiss it. Sometimes they kiss your palm while you're crying, or furious, or too tired to talk.

This one is less about wanting and more about staying. It asks for nothing. When someone holds your palm to their face, they're taking comfort as much as offering it, and the kiss is how they say so without interrupting whatever you're feeling.

Don't decode it. Leave your hand where it is.

5. The flirt: "Your move"

The playful version shows up early, usually at a bar or a party, usually with a pretext. The oldest one in the book is palm reading: they take your hand, trace your "life line" with a fingertip, predict something outrageous, and seal the fortune with a quick kiss in the middle.

It's a test, and a bold one. Because the palm is private, a palm kiss from someone new moves faster than a kiss on the back of the hand, and they're watching what you do next. Leave your hand in theirs and you've agreed to play. Take it back with a laugh and they can pretend it was part of the act.

Both are fine answers. If you can't tell whether the rest of the night is heading somewhere, the signs someone wants to kiss you will fill in the picture.

How to tell which palm kiss you got

When you're replaying the moment, these are the details that sort one kiss from another:

  • Speed. A quick press is a flirt or a keepsake. A long, still kiss is devotion. A slow kiss that moves is desire.
  • The eyes. Eyes up and on yours usually means desire or the flirt. Eyes closed usually means devotion or comfort.
  • Their hands. Cradling yours from underneath is care. Gripping your wrist is closer to a claim.
  • Your fingers. Folded closed over the kiss is the keepsake. Left open means they aren't finished.
  • Where it goes next. Up the wrist is desire. Back to ordinary hand-holding is affection. A step back and a goodbye is exactly that.
  • The setting. A crowded bar, your couch and an airport curb turn the same kiss into three different sentences.
  • Who they are to you. A partner of five years doing it while you're chopping onions is everyday devotion. A second date doing it is a statement.

No single clue settles it. Two or three pointing the same way usually do.

What a kiss on the wrist means

The inside of your wrist is where your pulse runs close to the surface. It's the spot a clinician presses to take your radial pulse, and the spot where plenty of people dab perfume. A kiss there is part kiss and part inhale, and it lands right on top of your heartbeat. Whether they can feel it speed up is between you and your pulse.

A kiss on the inside of the wrist usually means longing: slow down, I'm taking my time with you. It's rarely the first place anyone kisses. More often it's the second stop, after the palm, on the way up the arm. Remember what Archer had to do before he reached Ellen's palm: unbutton a glove. The wrist is the doorway, and someone who lingers in a doorway is savoring the approach.

From a partner, a slow wrist kiss with eyes up is an invitation. A quick one while you're walking hand in hand is pure affection, the wrist's answer to a kiss on the shoulder. And if the trail keeps going past the elbow, you know where it's headed. The neck is the wrist's louder cousin, and a kiss on the neck is rarely just friendly.

What it means from a guy, a girl, a date or a partner

People search what it means when a guy kisses your palm, and separately what it means when a girl does. The decoder above doesn't change with gender. Speed, eyes, hands and fingers read the same on everyone. What changes the meaning is who they are to you.

  • From someone you just met: almost always the flirt, and a confident one. You're allowed to find it charming, and you're allowed to take your hand back.
  • From someone you're dating: usually desire, sometimes the flirt. Either way, they're telling you where they'd like this to go.
  • From a partner: any of the five, and the moment usually makes it obvious. The keepsake and the comfort kiss belong almost entirely to people who already share a life.
  • From a friend: rare and loaded. Plenty of friends kiss cheeks; very few kiss palms. If a friend just did, the friendship may be changing, and how to kiss a friend covers what happens if you both want it to.

How to respond to a kiss on the palm

Here's what nobody tells you: the best answer to a palm kiss is usually your hand, not your mouth.

If you liked it

Don't yank your hand back out of surprise. Let your fingers relax. Let them curl a little toward their jaw, or rest your thumb on their cheek. That tiny movement says yes, keep going more clearly than anything you could say out loud.

If you want to answer in kind, take their hand, turn it over slowly, and give the kiss back. Few moves say "message received" as fluently.

If it's too much, too soon

Take your hand back gently, with a squeeze on the way out and a smile if you mean it kindly. "Let's slow down" works too. The palm is private territory. You decide who gets in, and when.

If it was a keepsake

Close your hand. That's the whole answer. (Pressing it to your cheek later is optional, and nobody needs to know.)

How to kiss someone's palm

Maybe you're on the other side of the question and want to give one. One rule first: a palm kiss is for someone who is already comfortable with your touch. You've held hands, they lean into you, they reach for you as often as you reach for them. It is not an icebreaker for a stranger or a coworker.

1. Start with ordinary hand-holding

Palm kisses don't come out of nowhere. Hold their hand the normal way for a while first. The contrast is part of the effect.

2. Turn their hand over slowly

Take a full second. A slow turn is a question, and their hand relaxing into it is the answer. If their hand stiffens or pulls away, stop and simply hold it. You got your answer, and it arrived politely.

3. Support it from underneath

Cradle the back of their hand in your fingers so their palm rests open and easy. Supporting a hand from below reads as care; clamping it by the wrist reads as possession.

4. Look up first

Meet their eyes before your lips arrive. Those two seconds of eye contact do the asking for you.

5. Kiss the center and hold one beat

Closed lips, relaxed mouth, a light press into the middle of the palm. No tongue and nothing wet; the palm notices everything, saliva included. Hold it for a second or two, then let your exhale brush their skin as you lift away.

6. Decide what their fingers do

Fold them closed over the kiss if you're saying goodbye. Leave them open and move to the inside of the wrist if the moment is heading somewhere. Or lower their hand slowly and keep holding it. All three are complete sentences.

A bonus: this is the one kiss a height difference can't complicate, because you bring the hand to you instead of folding your whole body down. Palm to palm

The most famous palm kiss in literature happens right before a first kiss.

In Act 1, Scene 5 of Romeo and Juliet, Romeo takes Juliet's hand at her family's party and offers to smooth his rough touch with a kiss. Juliet tells him hands are holy enough:

For saints have hands that pilgrims' hands do touch, / And palm to palm is holy palmers' kiss.

A palmer was a pilgrim who came home from the Holy Land carrying a palm leaf, and Juliet's pun is doing a lot of flirting. Romeo, being Romeo, talks his way to her lips by the end of the sonnet.

Juliet understood the palm. It's where a kiss goes when it isn't sure it's allowed any further, and where it goes when there's more to say than a mouth can manage. Read the speed, the eyes and what happens to your fingers. Then decide whether to close your hand around the kiss or reach for their face.

Either way, someone chose the side of your hand that nobody else gets. That's worth noticing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a kiss on the palm mean?

A kiss on the palm usually means desire, devotion or both, from someone who already feels close to you. The back of the hand is the polite, public side of a hand kiss; turning your hand over to kiss the palm is a deliberately private choice. Depending on the moment, it can also be a keepsake goodbye, a wordless comfort or a bold flirt.

What does it mean when a guy kisses your palm?

It means the same thing it means from anyone: he's closer, or wants to be closer, than a polite hand kiss allows. A slow kiss with eye contact that drifts toward your wrist is desire. A still kiss with his eyes closed is devotion. A quick one at a bar is a flirt, and he's watching what you do with your hand next.

What does it mean when a girl kisses your palm?

The same as from anyone. A slow palm kiss with eye contact usually means she wants more; a quiet, still one means she cares deeply; a quick one while she's teasing you is a flirt. If she folds your fingers over the kiss before a goodbye, she's giving you something to keep.

Is a kiss on the palm more intimate than a kiss on the hand?

Yes. A kiss on the back of the hand can be pure etiquette, the kind of gesture people make at weddings and formal dinners. The palm is the private side of the hand, and it's made of the same hairless, receptor-rich skin as your lips and fingertips, so the kiss is both more personal and more noticeable.

What does a kiss on the wrist mean?

A kiss on the inside of the wrist usually means longing or desire. It lands right over the spot where your pulse runs close to the surface, and it's often the second stop after a palm kiss, on the way up the arm. A quick kiss on the wrist while you're holding hands is simply affection.

What does it mean when someone kisses your palm and closes your hand?

It's a keepsake kiss: a kiss given to you to hold on to. It usually happens at a goodbye and means something like "I'll miss you; carry this until I'm back." The gesture is best known from Audrey Penn's picture book The Kissing Hand, and the right response is to close your hand around it.

How do you kiss someone's palm?

Hold their hand first, then turn it over slowly and support it from underneath. Meet their eyes, kiss the center of the palm with closed, relaxed lips, and hold for a second or two. Only do it with someone who is already comfortable with your touch, and stop if their hand pulls away.
